# Nonrivalness

**Nonrivalness** is the property; [[nonrival-goods]] are the goods that have it. A nonrival good can be consumed by many people simultaneously without being used up — a digital file, a recipe, a theorem, a broadcast.

It is the middle point of the [[rivalry]] axis: use neither subtracts (as with [[rival-goods|rival]] goods) nor adds (as with [[antirivalness|antirival]] systems). Standard public-goods economics, following Samuelson, treats nonrivalness as one half of what makes a [[public-goods|public good]] (the other being non-excludability).

A useful distinction for co-goods: nonrivalness lowers the *cost* of sharing — you can distribute without depletion — but it does not by itself raise the *benefit*. A file can be copied endlessly and still sit unused. Turning low-cost sharing into high-value sharing is the work of [[antirivalness]]: embedding the nonrival components in systems that get better as more people take part.
