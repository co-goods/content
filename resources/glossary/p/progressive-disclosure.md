# Progressive disclosure

**Progressive disclosure** keeps an interface simple by revealing detail only as it is wanted: a short default view, with advanced options, deeper explanation, or finer controls held one layer back. The term comes from human-computer interaction (Jack Carroll's work, later popularised by the Nielsen Norman Group); the goal is to lower the cost of the common case without putting the depth out of reach.

For Co-Goods it is a navigation principle as much as a UI one. The knowledge base has to serve a newcomer and a deep contributor — and increasingly agents as well as people — so each page should present what something *is* and what to read next, letting the reader drill from purpose down to detail at their own pace (it pairs naturally with the [[abstraction-decomposition-space]]).
