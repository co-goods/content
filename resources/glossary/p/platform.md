# Platform

A **platform** makes its money by *orchestrating interactions* rather than producing goods: it connects two or more groups — producers and consumers, drivers and riders, developers and users — and takes a position in the value that flows between them. Because each new participant on one side makes the platform more valuable to the other, its core engine is [[network-effects|network effects]], and its defensibility is one of the classic kinds of [[moat]].

The platform model is the dominant business form of the networked economy (Parker, Van Alstyne, and Choudary's *Platform Revolution* is the standard account). The co-goods question is whose value it compounds: a conventional platform captures the surplus its network creates, while a co-goods system aims to route that surplus back to the participants who generate it — a platform whose [[antirivalness]] benefits the many rather than the owner.
