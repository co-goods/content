title: Physical vs. digital

comparison:
  terms:
    - physical
    - digital
  description:
    - Physical and digital are routinely set against each other, but they are not the same kind of category — so the contrast is a category error. Physical names a mode of *existence* (whether something is made of matter); digital names a mode of *representation* (how information is encoded). The clean opposition is physical vs. non-physical, and digital is just one kind of non-physical representation.
  detailed_comparison:
    - aspect: What kind of category it is
      term_a: A mode of existence — whether something is made of matter and occupies space.
      term_b: A mode of representation — how information is encoded (discrete, binary).
    - aspect: Its true opposite
      term_a: Non-physical.
      term_b: Analog (continuous representation).
    - aspect: How they relate
      term_a: Can store or carry information in either an analog or a digital form.
      term_b: A subset of the non-physical; still needs a physical substrate to exist.
not_to_be_confused_with:

relevance: What's rival is the physical unit; what's made antirival is the non-physical system — calling that system "digital" both over- and under-states it.
