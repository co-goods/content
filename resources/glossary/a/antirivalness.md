# Antirivalness

**Antirivalness** is the property; [[antirival-goods]] are the goods that have it. Where [[nonrivalness]] means *use doesn't subtract*, antirivalness means *use adds* — each additional participant makes the good more valuable for everyone, not less.

The idea was named in the digital-commons and open-source literature — Steven Weber's account of open-source production, and Lawrence Lessig's discussion of resources that improve with use — and developed by F. Xavier Olleros in his work on antirival goods. A language, a protocol, a knowledge commons, a network: all get better as more people join, through [[network-effects|network effects]] and accumulated contribution.

Antirivalness sits on the [[rivalry]] axis but points the opposite way from [[rival-goods|rival]]: it is not a tolerance of extra users but a *benefit* from them. The co-goods reading is that a physical product, rival at the unit level, can still be wrapped in a strongly antirival *system* — open designs, shared knowledge, participation, feedback — so the more people take part, the more the whole is worth.
