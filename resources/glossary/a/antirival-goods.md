# Antirival

Antirival goods (or, more precisely, antirival *systems*) become **more** valuable as more people use them — the opposite of [[rival-goods]] goods, where one person's use prevents another's. The concept is orthogonal to the classic rival / [[nonrival-goods]] distinction rather than a point on the same line: an antirival good doesn't merely tolerate additional users, it *benefits* from them through [[network-effects|network effects]].

Physical products are rival at the unit level, but a co-good can wrap a rival product in a strongly antirival *system* — maximising the nonrival components (designs, patterns, knowledge, feedback) and building participation that generates increasing returns.

The term was developed in the digital-commons literature (notably F. Xavier Olleros's work on antirival goods and the [[sharing-economy|sharing economy]]).
