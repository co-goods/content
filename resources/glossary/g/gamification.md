# Gamification

**Gamification** borrows the mechanics that make games compelling — clear goals, progression, feedback, rewards, status — and applies them outside games to encourage [[participation]] and sustained [[engagement]]. The quality of a gamified system turns on *which* motivation it engages: Yu-kai Chou's Octalysis framework distinguishes *white-hat* drives (meaning, mastery, autonomy) from *black-hat* ones (scarcity, unpredictability, loss-aversion) that compel in the short term but corrode trust.

For co-goods the distinction is the point. Engagement designed to empower participants — to make contributing rewarding in itself — fits an [[antirivalness|antirival]] system, where more participation makes the whole worth more. Engagement engineered to extract attention does the opposite.
