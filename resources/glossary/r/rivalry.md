# Rivalry

In the economic sense, **rivalry** is a property of a *good*, not a relationship between competitors. It asks one question: does my use of this thing subtract from what is left for you?

That question defines an axis. At one end sit [[rival-goods]] — a sandwich, a seat, a worn garment — where consumption is subtractive. In the middle are [[nonrival-goods]] — a file, a broadcast, an idea — used by many at once without depleting. At the far end are [[antirival-goods]], where use is *additive* (see [[antirivalness]]). Paired with excludability, this axis produces the classic [[goods-theory|typology of goods]] — [[private-goods]], [[public-goods]], and the rest.

Keep rivalry-the-axis distinct from *competitive* rivalry between firms: one is about whether a good is used up, the other about who wins a contest. Co-goods design works on the first — taking products that are rival at the unit level and wrapping them in nonrival and antirival systems so that more participants make the whole worth more.
